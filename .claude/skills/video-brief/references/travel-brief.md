# Match the story to the journey

Use for a travel film after setup, alongside tone and humor. Reuse known answers. Travel structure, output format and mood are independent: a roadtrip can be a serious reel; a stay in one place can be a funny long film. Do not force every journey into the previous roadtrip template.

| Journey | Editorial structure to consider | Useful follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Roadtrip | Departures, changing landscapes, shared car moments, arrivals and a destination payoff | Known stops, transport per leg, meaningful vehicle details |
| One base / one destination | Daily rhythms, activities, relationships and discoveries; local excursions | What made this place or shared time special? |
| Multi-stop journey | Chapters by place with the actual transport changes between them | Train/flight/ferry/car legs and uncertain gaps |
| Activity / expedition | Preparation, progression, challenge and return; setbacks only if they happened | Main activity and meaningful milestones |
| Mixed | Different phases within one film, e.g. several city days followed by driving | Where does each phase start and what changes? |
| Other / not travel | Follow the user's own structure | Skip irrelevant travel questions |

Ask a short opening question such as: “Roadtrip, mainly one place, a multi-stop journey, an activity trip, or a mixture? What should the film feel like?” If style is already known, ask only for the missing journey information. Offer free text and “unknown.” Then bundle only the consequential gaps: region, known route, whether the trip is planned/ongoing/already filmed, and transport. Mixed trips need phase-specific choices, not one vehicle assigned to every scene.

## The vehicle can be a recurring character

For driving chapters, ask vehicle type (sedan/SUV/convertible/camper/motorcycle/other), make/model **if known**, color and distinctive features if useful. Ask whether it deserves a visual motif or should remain incidental. A nickname, dashboard item or recurring packing ritual may be more personal than an exact model badge.

Preserve uncertainty explicitly; an approximate name is not a confirmed model. Do not guess a car from a place name or carry over a blue camper from an earlier project. A neutral silhouette of the confirmed body type is a usable fallback; color stays unresolved until supported. No license plate or rental paperwork is needed for the graphic.

Use a car motif through real details, original sounds and a fitting map icon. Change icons only at supported transport changes. A roadtrip with a city stay at the beginning does not prove the group drove around that city. Whether maps appear at all, once as an overview or between chapters is a separate preference. Broad waypoints justify a schematic overview, not an invented exact road or mileage.

## Apply the answers

- Planned trip: propose a small achievable shot/sound list. Do not claim shots or events exist. Suggest passenger/fixed-camera driving views and parked vehicle details rather than filming by the driver.
- Already filmed: inventory first and build from available moments. Missing coverage is an editorial constraint, not permission to invent visits or insert misleading stock footage.
- Translate journey type into chapter purpose, map frequency, sound motifs, people/landscape balance and suitable gags. Keep style/humor overrides intact. Spend screen time on meaningful material, not mechanically in proportion to days spent.
- For funny edits, build recurring jokes from observed rituals, then vary the payoff. For serious edits, the same vehicle/route motif can stay subtle. Neither a car choice nor a country implies a humor level.

Record `trip_type`, optional `trip_type_details`, `trip_stage`, `region`, `route`, `transport`, `vehicle`, `vehicle_role`, `map_preference` and `story_focus` separately from `mode`, `style`, `humor` and `format`. Route/transport prose should preserve leg-specific unknowns until a verified route table exists. Old briefs without these fields remain usable; clarify only what affects the next edit. Unresolved fields are notes, not a requirement to ask every question.
